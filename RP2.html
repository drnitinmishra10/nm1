1. Abstract

The rapid adoption of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in healthcare, finance, education, recruitment, public services and other high-impact domains has created an urgent need for reliable, fair and trustworthy AI systems. Although considerable research has focused on improving model architectures and predictive accuracy, a fundamental source of algorithmic harm often originates before model training: the data itself.

Machine learning datasets may contain class imbalance, demographic under-representation, sampling bias, label noise, distributional disparities, geographical bias and intersectional representation gaps [1,2,9]. These problems can remain hidden when conventional aggregate performance metrics such as accuracy and F1-score are used. Consequently, a model may demonstrate high overall accuracy while performing substantially worse for specific underrepresented groups.

The proposed project introduces FairDataGuard, an adaptive data-centric framework for automated dataset auditing, quantitative bias assessment, mitigation and downstream validation. The framework will systematically analyze multiple dimensions of dataset bias and generate an interpretable Multi-Dimensional Bias Risk Index (MBRI). Based on the identified bias profile, FairDataGuard will recommend and evaluate suitable mitigation strategies including adaptive re-sampling, re-weighting, augmentation, synthetic data generation and bias-aware preprocessing.

Unlike conventional approaches that apply a single mitigation technique irrespective of dataset characteristics, FairDataGuard will investigate an adaptive decision mechanism that selects mitigation strategies according to the observed bias structure and evaluates the resulting trade-off between fairness, predictive performance and robustness.

The framework will be validated using multiple benchmark datasets, with particular emphasis on high-impact healthcare and dermatological image datasets where representation and skin-tone-related disparities have emerged as important concerns in AI-based diagnosis. The project will deliver a functional prototype, validated bias-auditing methodology, adaptive mitigation algorithms, benchmark results, research publications and potential intellectual property.

3. Background and Rationale

Machine Learning systems are increasingly being used for decision-making and prediction in socially important domains. However, predictive accuracy alone is insufficient to establish that an AI system is reliable or trustworthy.

A model trained on an unrepresentative dataset can inherit and amplify deficiencies present in the training data [2,9]. These deficiencies may arise from class imbalance, demographic under-representation, geographical concentration, sampling bias, inconsistent annotation, distribution shift, missing demographic groups and intersectional representation gaps.

Dermatological AI provides a particularly important validation setting because representation and skin-tone-related performance disparities remain an active research challenge [7,8,10]. This motivates a data-centric approach in which bias is explicitly measured before and after model training.

Core research principle: Fair AI begins with fair, representative and appropriately characterized data.

4. Problem Statement

Current machine learning workflows generally focus on data preprocessing followed by model development. Dataset auditing is often performed through disconnected statistical analyses, while bias mitigation is commonly applied using predefined techniques.

  1. Multiple types of dataset bias are rarely analyzed within one integrated framework.
  2. There is limited availability of interpretable dataset-level bias indicators.
  3. The most appropriate mitigation strategy depends on the specific bias profile of a dataset.
  4. Reduction in statistical imbalance does not necessarily translate into improved model-level fairness.
  5. Bias mitigation may improve subgroup performance while degrading overall predictive performance.

Central research problem: How can an intelligent data-centric framework automatically identify, quantify, diagnose and mitigate multiple forms of dataset bias while preserving predictive performance and improving group-wise robustness?

5. Research Gap

5.1 Fragmented Bias Detection

Existing approaches frequently investigate individual forms of bias such as class imbalance, demographic bias or sampling bias separately.

5.2 Lack of Unified Dataset-Level Risk Assessment

Individual statistical indicators do not provide a consolidated representation of the overall bias risk associated with a dataset.

5.3 Limited Adaptive Mitigation

Most mitigation techniques are selected manually. A dataset requiring re-weighting may be unnecessarily subjected to oversampling, potentially introducing additional artifacts.

5.4 Bias–Performance Trade-Off

Reducing disparity does not automatically mean improving the complete ML system. Fairness must be evaluated simultaneously with predictive performance and robustness.

5.5 Intersectional Bias

Bias may become more severe when multiple characteristics interact. A subgroup defined by combinations of attributes may be substantially underrepresented even when individual attributes appear reasonably balanced.

5.6 Limited End-to-End Validation

A dataset-level intervention should ultimately be evaluated through downstream model performance, subgroup performance, robustness and statistical significance.

6. Aim

To design, develop and validate FairDataGuard, an adaptive data-centric framework capable of auditing, quantifying and mitigating multiple forms of machine learning dataset bias while improving fairness, robustness and generalization of downstream AI models.

7. Research Objectives

  1. Develop a systematic taxonomy and computational representation of major dataset bias dimensions.
  2. Develop automated algorithms for detecting representation, class, sampling, label, distributional and intersectional bias.
  3. Develop a normalized Multi-Dimensional Bias Risk Index (MBRI) for quantitative dataset-level risk assessment.
  4. Develop an adaptive mitigation recommendation engine that selects appropriate data-level interventions according to the detected bias profile.
  5. Investigate re-sampling, re-weighting, augmentation, synthetic data generation and bias-aware preprocessing techniques.
  6. Develop a multi-objective optimization mechanism balancing fairness, predictive performance and robustness.
  7. Validate the framework across multiple benchmark datasets and ML architectures.
  8. Develop a functional software prototype capable of producing an automated dataset fairness audit report.

8. Research Questions

  1. Can multiple dimensions of dataset bias be automatically detected using a unified computational framework?
  2. Can a normalized Multi-Dimensional Bias Risk Index reliably characterize dataset bias?
  3. Can mitigation strategies be selected automatically from the observed bias profile?
  4. Can data-level interventions reduce subgroup performance disparities without unacceptable degradation in overall model performance?
  5. Does the proposed framework generalize across different datasets and machine learning models?
  6. Can the proposed framework improve fairness while maintaining robustness under distributional variation?

9. Proposed FairDataGuard Framework

Dataset Input→ Data Quality & Representation Auditor→ Multi-Dimensional Bias Detection→ MBRI Generator→ Adaptive Mitigation→ Model Validation→ Fairness Audit Report

The generated report will identify detected bias types, severity, affected groups, intersectional vulnerabilities, recommended mitigation, before/after bias measurements, subgroup performance, overall performance, robustness indicators and statistical significance.

10. Bias Dimensions

10.1 Class Imbalance

Analysis using imbalance ratio, entropy, Gini-based measures and class-frequency statistics.

10.2 Representation Bias

Identification of underrepresented groups, attributes and classes.

10.3 Sampling Bias

Comparison of sample distributions against available reference distributions.

10.4 Label Bias

Detection of inconsistent, noisy or systematically different labeling patterns.

10.5 Distributional Bias

Measurement of differences between training, validation and evaluation distributions.

10.6 Intersectional Bias

Analysis of combinations of attributes to identify hidden subgroup vulnerabilities.

10.7 Image/Data Quality Bias

Where applicable, analysis of image quality, acquisition conditions, illumination, resolution and other data-generation characteristics that may correlate with particular groups.

11. Multi-Dimensional Bias Risk Index

A key research contribution will be the development of the Multi-Dimensional Bias Risk Index (MBRI).

MBRI = Σ wi × Bi

where Bi is the normalized bias indicator for dimension i and wi is an adaptive importance weight. The final formulation and thresholds will be empirically established through experimental validation rather than predetermined arbitrarily.

The index will provide dimension-wise risk, aggregate bias risk, dominant bias identification, intersectional risk indicators and before/after mitigation comparison.

12. Adaptive Mitigation Engine

FairDataGuard will investigate an adaptive strategy-selection mechanism. Candidate interventions will include:

  • Random oversampling and undersampling
  • Stratified and adaptive sampling
  • Cluster-based sampling
  • Class and instance re-weighting
  • SMOTE and suitable variants
  • Controlled data augmentation
  • Synthetic data generation
  • Bias-aware preprocessing
Adaptive principle: Detected Bias Profile → Candidate Mitigation Strategies → Experimental Evaluation → Multi-Objective Selection.

13. Multi-Objective Optimization

The project will explicitly model three competing objectives, reflecting the need to consider fairness alongside model utility and robustness [1,2,6].

  • Fairness: Reduction in subgroup performance disparity.
  • Predictive Performance: Maintenance or improvement of accuracy, F1-score, ROC-AUC and PR-AUC.
  • Robustness: Stable performance across datasets, subgroups and distributional variations.

The framework will therefore seek a solution that does not simply maximize fairness at the expense of model utility.

25. Ethical and Responsible AI Framework

  • Use public or appropriately licensed datasets.
  • Protect sensitive information and anonymize where necessary.
  • Use demographic attributes responsibly and only when scientifically justified.
  • Report limitations transparently.
  • Maintain reproducible experiments.
  • Avoid discriminatory deployment.
  • Interpret fairness metrics in context.
  • Keep research validation separate from clinical decision-making.

The project does not assume that all demographic differences represent bias. The objective is to identify unintended, unjustified or harmful disparities arising from data and machine learning processes.

33. Conclusion

The proposed FairDataGuard project addresses a critical challenge in contemporary Artificial Intelligence: the development of machine learning systems that are not only accurate, but also reliable, robust and fair.

Rather than treating bias as a problem that appears only after model training, the project introduces a data-centric Responsible AI approach in which datasets are systematically audited, bias is quantified, mitigation strategies are adaptively selected, and resulting changes are validated at the model level.

The central scientific contribution will be the integration of multi-dimensional bias auditing, quantitative risk assessment, adaptive mitigation and fairness-performance-robustness optimization within a single reusable framework.

The project is technically feasible within a 12-month period and a proposed budget of ₹5,00,000 using existing institutional infrastructure supplemented by targeted manpower and computational resources.

The resulting FairDataGuard prototype, algorithms, benchmark results, publications and potential intellectual property can form the foundation for a larger research programme in Responsible AI, Fair Machine Learning and Data-Centric Artificial Intelligence.
