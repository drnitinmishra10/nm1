Engineering Excellence & Transformation Plan

A strategic improvement framework for CGC University, Mohali — designed for the Dean Engineering role
Student-Centric Industry-Connected Outcome-Based Research-Driven Data-Informed
Vision: Build an engineering ecosystem in which curriculum, classroom learning, laboratories, industry exposure, internships, placements and research operate as one continuous improvement cycle.

Executive Strategy

The improvement plan is built around eight interconnected priorities. The objective is not to increase activities merely for the sake of activity, but to improve measurable student outcomes, faculty capability, industry engagement, academic quality and research impact.

1. Curriculum Design & Development

Strategic Goal

Create curricula that preserve strong engineering fundamentals while responding quickly to industry, technology and societal requirements.

Curriculum Council:
Programme faculty + industry experts + alumni + students + academic leadership.
Annual Curriculum Review:
Review course relevance, prerequisites, electives, laboratory content and employability skills.
Emerging Technology:
AI/ML, Data Science, Cybersecurity, Cloud, IoT, Robotics, Automation, Sustainable Engineering and other relevant domains.
Applied Components:
Increase live projects, laboratories, internships, design challenges and capstone work.

Key KPIs

  • 100% programmes undergo structured curriculum review annually.
  • Each programme obtains documented industry/alumni feedback.
  • Identify at least 5–10 curriculum improvement actions per programme, based on evidence.
  • Map priority courses to employability competencies and POs/PSOs.

2. Student Placement

Strategic Goal

Move from a placement-event model to a year-round employability ecosystem.

  • Develop branch-wise recruiter maps: IT, core engineering, consulting, manufacturing, infrastructure, electronics and emerging technology sectors.
  • Build stronger relationships with employers for internships, live projects, recruitment and curriculum inputs.
  • Track not only placement percentage but median compensation, recruiter diversity, core/non-core placement, internship conversion and higher-study outcomes.
  • Create an early-warning system for students who are not placement-ready.

KPIs

  • 100% graduating students receive a placement-readiness assessment.
  • Quarterly recruiter and skill-gap review.
  • Increase the number of active recruiters and core-engineering opportunities based on the established baseline.
  • Track quality and sustainability of placements rather than relying on one headline percentage.

3. Classroom Engagement

Strategic Goal

Move from predominantly lecture-centred delivery to active, participatory and experiential learning.

PracticeImplementation
Problem-Based LearningReal engineering problems introduced within relevant courses.
Project-Based LearningStudents develop demonstrable solutions, prototypes or software.
Case StudiesIndustry and real-world cases integrated into teaching.
Flipped/Blended LearningPre-class digital resources followed by application-focused classroom work.
Interactive AssessmentQuizzes, polls, demonstrations, peer instruction and short formative assessments.
Industry ClassroomPractitioners contribute examples, problems and expert sessions.

KPI: Pilot active-learning practices in at least 20–30% of identified priority courses in Year One, then scale based on evidence.

4. Teaching-Learning Practices

The Dean's Teaching Quality Cycle

PLAN: Course outcomes, lesson planning, resources, assessment design and student prerequisites.
DELIVER: Engaging teaching, practical work, demonstrations, digital resources and differentiated support.
ASSESS: Formative and summative assessment, CO attainment and student feedback.
IMPROVE: Gap analysis, remedial actions, advanced learning and curriculum/teaching revision.
  • Faculty peer-learning and teaching-observation mechanisms.
  • Remedial support for identified learning gaps.
  • Advanced/accelerated opportunities for high-performing students.
  • Consistent use of LMS and digital academic resources.

5. Outcome Based Education (OBE)

Strategic Goal

Make OBE a genuine academic quality system rather than a documentation exercise.

CO → CO–PO/PSO Mapping → Assessment → Attainment → Gap Analysis → Corrective Action → Reassessment
  • 100% programmes maintain a current CO–PO–PSO mapping framework.
  • Each programme conducts periodic attainment analysis.
  • Document at least one evidence-based continuous-improvement action per programme each review cycle.
  • Use student performance, feedback, industry input and assessment data to refine teaching and curriculum.

6. Credit Structure of an Engineering Programme

Strategic Goal

Ensure that credits represent meaningful learning workload and contribute to graduate competencies, while complying with applicable regulatory and university academic requirements.

A sound engineering structure should balance:

Foundation: Mathematics, sciences and humanities/social sciences.
Engineering Fundamentals: Basic engineering sciences and foundational engineering practice.
Professional Core: Essential discipline-specific knowledge.
Professional Electives: Specialisation and emerging technology choices.
Open/Interdisciplinary Electives: Cross-disciplinary exposure.
Applied Learning: Laboratories, internships, projects, seminar/capstone.

Dean-Level Credit Audit

  • Check whether credit distribution supports programme outcomes and graduate attributes.
  • Check balance between theory, practical work, electives, internship and capstone.
  • Review prerequisites and course sequencing.
  • Identify unnecessary duplication or gaps.
  • Ensure alignment with the applicable AICTE/UGC/NCrF framework and university academic regulations.

Principle: “Credits should be treated as a mechanism for meaningful learning and flexibility, not simply as numbers to be accumulated.”

8. Faculty Research & Development

Strategic Goal

Move from isolated publication activity towards collaborative, funded, industry-relevant and translational research.

Research Clusters:
AI/Data Science, Cybersecurity, IoT/Robotics, Advanced Computing, Smart Manufacturing, Sustainable Engineering and other areas aligned with faculty strengths.
Funding:
Grant identification, proposal mentoring, internal seed support and collaborative proposals.
Industry Research:
Convert industry problems into consultancy, sponsored R&D and student research projects.
Innovation:
Patents, prototypes, technology transfer, startups and societal applications.

Year-One KPIs

  • Establish 5–8 interdisciplinary research clusters, subject to faculty strength.
  • Each cluster develops 2–3 fundable or industry-relevant proposals.
  • Conduct at least 2 grant/proposal development workshops.
  • Map at least 10 industry/research problem statements for potential projects.
  • Track quality publications, external funding, patents, consultancy, prototypes and student research participation.

Leadership Principles

1. Measure Outcomes, Not Activities
An MoU matters only when it generates meaningful engagement.
2. Improve Before Expanding
Strengthen existing systems before creating additional layers of administration.
3. Faculty as Partners
Use consultation, mentoring and evidence rather than top-down change.
4. Students as Stakeholders
Use student feedback and performance data in academic decisions.
5. Industry as a Learning Partner
Industry should contribute to curriculum, projects, internships, research and recruitment.
6. Continuous Improvement
Every major initiative should have a baseline, target, review cycle and corrective action.

Most importantly, I would measure outcomes rather than activities and use evidence for continuous improvement.”
